# Allergen Guide, Procedures and Compliance: England and Scotland

*Prepared from the FSA, FSS and GOV.UK source documents in your project. Last source check: 22 November 2026. This is a compliance reference, not legal advice; confirm specifics with your local authority.*

## 1. Which rules apply where

The underlying law is shared, but the guidance, tools and enforcement are run by different agencies. Always cite the correct one.

|  | **England** | **Scotland** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agency | Food Standards Agency (FSA), guidance on GOV.UK | Food Standards Scotland (FSS) |
| Core law | Food Information to Consumers (FIC), Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 as assimilated law | Same FIC regulation, applied through Scottish enforcement rules |
| Enforcement rules | Food Information Regulations 2014 (FIR), as amended (including the 2022 England amendment) | Scottish equivalent of the 2014 regulations (FSS summary guidance covers Scotland, Wales and NI) |
| Main guidance | FSA *Food allergen labelling and information requirements technical guidance* (updated 3 Nov 2026) | FSS *Managing allergens*, *Allergen labelling*, *Prepacked for direct sale* pages |
| Checklists and signs | FSA *Allergen checklist for food businesses* (updated 23 Jul 2026) | Use FSS tools and CookSafe allergen records; FSA checklist is explicitly England, NI and Wales |
| Training | FSA free online food allergy training (6 modules, about 3 hours) | FSS free Online Allergen Training plus Advanced Online Allergen Training |
| Business tool | FSA icons, matrix and posters | FSS MenuCal (allergens, calories, PPDS labels) |
| Extra Scottish duties | None equivalent | Must report breaches of food information law to FSS (Food (Scotland) Act 2015); enforcement can seize or hold food for suspected breaches |

**Rule of thumb:** a business in England (also Wales and NI for FSA material) uses the FSA documents. A business in Scotland uses the FSS documents. The 14-allergen list, the PPDS labelling rule and the duty to give accurate information are the same across the UK.

## 2. Legal foundation

- **FIC, Article 9(1)(c):** every food business must declare any of the 14 allergens used as an ingredient or processing aid that remains in the final food.
- **Articles 12 and 13:** information must be easily accessible, conspicuous, clearly legible and not obscured. Information must be accurate however it is provided.
- **Article 21:** how allergens are emphasised in ingredient lists.
- **Article 14:** distance selling (phone, online, takeaway).
- **Article 44:** national measures for non-prepacked food.
- **Annex II:** the list of 14 allergens.
- **PPDS rule (in force 1 October 2021, often called Natasha’s Law):** applies in all four UK nations.
- The FIC applies to all food supplied by food businesses, including food offered free. Occasional charity or community providers may also be covered; check with the local authority.

## 3. The 14 regulated allergens

| # | Allergen | Scope and common sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Celery | Stalks, leaves, seeds, celeriac, celery salt; found in soups, stock cubes, some meat products |
| 2 | Cereals containing gluten | Wheat (including spelt, Khorasan/Kamut), rye, barley, oats; batter, breadcrumbs, pastry, sauces, flour-dusted fried food |
| 3 | Crustaceans | Prawns, crab, lobster, crayfish, langoustine; shrimp paste, shellfish stock |
| 4 | Eggs | Eggs from all birds; mayonnaise, mousses, pasta, glazes |
| 5 | Fish | All finned fish, fish oils, caviar; fish sauce, Worcestershire sauce, Caesar dressing, stock cubes |
| 6 | Lupin | Lupin flour and seeds; some breads, pastries, pasta |
| 7 | Milk | All mammalian milk, including lactose; butter, cheese, cream, yoghurt, glazes, powdered soups |
| 8 | Molluscs | Mussels, oysters, scallops, snails, squid, octopus; oyster sauce, paella |
| 9 | Mustard | All forms; dressings, curries, marinades, meat products |
| 10 | Peanuts | A legume, not a tree nut; satay, biscuits, groundnut oil. Use the word “peanut”, not “groundnut” |
| 11 | Sesame | Seeds, powder, oil, tahini; buns, hummus |
| 12 | Soybeans | Soya, tofu, edamame, miso, soya flour; labelled with a clear soya reference |
| 13 | Sulphur dioxide and sulphites | Declare above 10 mg/kg or 10 mg/litre in the finished food as consumed; dried fruit, wine, beer, soft drinks |
| 14 | Tree nuts | Almond, hazelnut, walnut, cashew, pecan, Brazil, pistachio, macadamia. Chestnut, pine nut and coconut are not on the list and must not be emphasised as allergens |

**Points to remember**

- Only these 14 are legally required, but customers may react to other foods. Staff should still take other allergy questions seriously.
- Some highly refined ingredients are exempt (for example fully refined soya oil, wheat glucose syrup). Check Annex II exemptions in the FSA technical guidance, paragraph 19.
- Cooking does not remove allergens. Hygiene, separation, storage and labelling are what protect customers.

## 4. How information must be provided, by type of food

| Food type | What is required | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Prepacked** | Full ingredient list with the 14 allergens emphasised every time they appear (bold, underline, contrasting colour, capitals). Nutrition declaration for most prepacked foods | Factory-packed products |
| **Prepacked for direct sale (PPDS)** | Name of the food plus full ingredient list with allergens emphasised | Sandwich wrapped on site before ordering; boxed salad; wrapped deli items; packaged cake samples |
| **Non-prepacked (loose)** | Allergen information for each dish, available before purchase. May be written (menu, chalkboard, matrix) or oral, but oral requires clear written signposting | Restaurant meals, takeaways, deli counter, food packed at the customer’s request |
| **Distance selling** | Allergen information available free of charge before the order is completed and again at delivery | Online, phone and delivery orders |

**Is it PPDS?** All three must be true: the food is in packaging that cannot be altered without opening it; it was packed before the customer ordered or selected it; and it was packed by the same business at the same premises or site (or the same mobile or temporary premises). Food wrapped after the customer orders it is not PPDS, and neither is a hot dog on an open tray. Food packed by one business and sold by another is ordinary prepacked food.

**Emphasis rules (prepacked and PPDS)**

- Name the allergen source clearly, for example “prawns (crustaceans)”, “tahini (sesame)”, “ghee (milk)”, “tofu (soya)”, “spelt (wheat)”.
- In multi-word ingredients, emphasise only the allergen word (for example skimmed **milk** powder, **egg** white).
- Do not repeat mandatory allergen information in a separate “Contains” line when an ingredient list is present. “Contains” is used where no ingredient list is required (tiny packs under 10 cm², alcoholic drinks).
- Minimum font x-height 1.2 mm where the label surface is 80 cm² or more.
- An allergy advice line such as “for allergens, see ingredients in bold” is allowed.

**Distance selling to PPDS:** the ingredient list requirement does not apply to PPDS food sold only by distance means; the Article 14 duty (information before purchase and at delivery) applies instead.

## 5. England-specific guidance (FSA; also applies to Wales and NI)

1. **Primary document:** FSA technical guidance, updated 3 November 2026. It combines regulatory compliance and best practice. Best practice is marked and is not a legal requirement.
2. **Non-prepacked food:** written information is best practice, supported by a conversation with the customer. Allergen information is needed for the whole dish as served. For buffets, give it for each item separately.
3. **Oral information:** allowed, but a notice, menu, ticket or label must signpost where to get it. This includes drive-through.
4. **Icons and matrix:** icons are permitted only with words or numbers alongside to avoid misleading customers. Minimum size 0.6 cm x 0.6 cm (similar to 20 pt type). Use black and white versions on monochrome printers. Download fresh each time rather than copy and paste. A matrix must show all 14 allergens present in each dish. Group symbols (nuts, gluten cereals) are allowed but staff must be able to name the specific nut or cereal on request.
5. **Signage:** FSA “Food Allergy or Intolerance?” sign (colour and black and white), plus posters in English (and Welsh for Wales).
6. **Checklists:** FSA allergen checklist covers managers, kitchen staff, front of house and delivery or takeaway.
7. **Training:** free FSA online training, six modules, pass all six tests for a certificate. The certificate is educational only and is not accreditation. Inactive accounts (2 years) may be deleted.
8. **Enforcement:** local authority food officers. In areas with county and district councils, county councils must enforce the FIR and districts may.

## 6. Scotland-specific guidance (FSS)

1. **Primary pages:** FSS *Managing allergens*, *Allergen labelling* and *Prepacked for direct sale*.
2. **Non-prepacked rule in force since December 2014:** providers must be able to tell customers about the 14 allergens in food served. Information may be on a menu, chalkboard or shelf label, or given verbally by staff, before purchase.
3. **PPDS in Scotland:** same rule since 1 October 2021. FSS provides an “Is my business affected?” questionnaire, a PPDS legislation toolkit and MenuCal PPDS label functionality.
4. **MenuCal:** free, secure online tool to organise recipes, manage allergen and calorie data and produce PPDS labels. Support: menucal@fss.scot, 01224 285100.
5. **CookSafe (HACCP):** includes Allergen Management records (updates dated February 2009 and September 2021 listed) and a cross-contamination prevention section. Use alongside RetailSafe or ButcherSafe where relevant.
6. **Training:** FSS free Online Allergen Training and Advanced Online Allergen Training (five sections: food allergies explained, rules and regulations, providing allergen information, allergen management, your responsibility as a business). Either can be taken independently. Staff must be trained on allergens.
7. **Extra Scottish duties:** since 1 April 2015 businesses must report any breach of food information law to FSS, and failure to do so is an offence. Since January 2016 enforcement authorities can seize or hold food suspected of breaching the rules; a sheriff may order a label corrected instead of the food being removed.
8. **Hygiene scheme:** Food Hygiene Information Scheme (Pass, Improvement Required, Awaiting Inspection, Exempt), with right of reply, appeal and re-visit request.
9. **Egg safety (relevant to egg allergen handling and Salmonella):** FSS egg guidance recommends UK hen eggs from British Lion Code or Laid in Britain schemes, pasteurised egg for raw or lightly cooked dishes for high-risk consumers, same-day use of pooled egg, and thorough cooking of duck, goose and quail eggs.

## 7. Operating procedures

These combine the FSA checklist, FSS management guidance and FSA technical guidance. They suit both countries; use the agency tools and training named above for your location.

### 7.1 Management

- Name one person with overall responsibility for allergen management, and make sure a responsible person is on every shift.
- Display the allergy sign where customers make food choices. Add a statement on printed menus, flyers and websites explaining how to get allergen information.
- Train all staff on allergens at induction and refresh annually. Record completion.
- Keep a written, current allergen record for every dish (recipe sheet or matrix).
- Set a procedure for recipe or supplier changes: approve, record, update the allergen record, notify front of house, and withdraw outdated information.
- Verify deliveries against orders and record any substitutions before use.
- Set cleaning instructions for premises, equipment and tools.

### 7.2 Receiving and storage

- Store ingredients in sealed, labelled containers; keep open packs sealed.
- If decanting from original packaging, keep a way to identify the allergens (label or retained pack information).
- Keep a spillage policy for one ingredient falling onto another.
- Record brand-specific allergens: allergens differ between brands. Use the MenuCal Supplier Information Sheet and recheck regularly.

### 7.3 Preparation

- Work from accurate recipe cards that list allergens (MenuCal recipe sheet or equivalent).
- Wash hands with soap and hot water before preparing allergen-free meals.
- Clean surfaces so no debris or crumbs remain; use clean utensils and separate equipment where possible.
- Check allergens in garnishes, toppings, sauces and dressings.
- Have a clear process so an allergic customer’s order is prepared safely and reaches the right customer. Check at the pass.
- Some allergens cannot be removed once part of the recipe (for example butter in a parfait); staff must know this.

### 7.4 Front of house

- Ask or prompt customers about allergies at order. Give accurate information from written records, not memory.
- Pass allergy requests to the kitchen in writing and flag them clearly on tickets.
- Tell customers about cross-contact risks.
- If unsure, refer the question to the responsible person. Never guess.

### 7.5 Delivery, takeaway and online

- Show a statement on the website about where to find allergen information, available before ordering and free to access.
- Provide written allergen information at delivery (stickers, a menu with cross-references, or a note) and clearly mark the allergen-free meal.
- Remove cross-contamination risks during packing and delivery.
- Follow the requirements of any third-party ordering platform.

### 7.6 PPDS labelling

1. Identify every item packed on site before sale.
2. Create the label: name of food plus full ingredients, allergens emphasised each time.
3. Verify against the current recipe and supplier data.
4. Reprint whenever ingredients change.
5. Review at least when recipes, brands or suppliers change.

## 8. Free-from, gluten and precautionary labelling

- **“Gluten-free”:** no more than 20 mg/kg gluten; **“very low gluten”:** no more than 100 mg/kg. These rules apply to restaurant food too. No other gluten-absence wording is permitted. Specially produced oats must still be declared and emphasised as oats.
- **Allergen “free from” claims** are a guarantee of absence and require strict controls. They must not be combined with a precautionary label for the same allergen.
- **Precautionary allergen labelling (“may contain”):** voluntary. Use only after a risk assessment shows an unavoidable cross-contamination risk that cannot be controlled. It does not replace good hygiene. Name the specific allergen (“may contain peanuts”, not “may contain nuts”).
- **NGCI statements** (“non-gluten containing ingredients”): best practice is to avoid them. If used, state they are not suitable for people with coeliac disease.
- Coeliac disease is autoimmune, not an allergy. Reactions can be delayed by hours and last days, but must still be taken seriously.

## 9. Responding to an allergic reaction (staff aide-memoire)

> **Important:** the response flow below comes from the sample guidance in your project (“Allergen Emergency Protocols”), which is demo material and is marked as sample guidance. It is not an official FSA or FSS document. Staff must follow the venue’s official first-aid procedure, and NHS anaphylaxis guidance is the reference the FSA checklist points to.

1. **Stop and stay.** Keep the guest calm, seated and never alone.
2. **Medication.** Ask whether they carry an adrenaline auto-injector or antihistamine and help them use it.
3. **Severe signs: call 999.** Say “anaphylaxis”. Lie them flat with legs raised, or sit them up if breathing is hard.
4. **Record.** Note what they ate and when. Keep the ticket. Tell the manager.

**Mild signs:** itchy mouth, hives, flushed skin, stomach pain. **Severe signs:** swelling of lips, face or throat, wheeze, trouble breathing, dizziness, collapse.

## 10. Enforcement and penalties

- Non-compliance can lead to an improvement notice or criminal prosecution.
- Breach of the allergen labelling provisions (FIR regulation 10) is a criminal offence; a conviction can bring a potentially unlimited fine set by the court.
- Scotland adds the duty to report breaches to FSS and powers to seize or hold food.
- Serving an allergic customer a food they are allergic to can cause serious harm and carry reputational, financial and potentially criminal consequences.

- [ ] Written allergen record held and current for every menu item
- [ ] Signage displayed where customers choose food, and menus explain how to ask
- [ ] Staff trained and records kept; annual refresh

## 13. Gaps and cautions in the source material

1. **FSA technical guidance review date.** The document shows an “updated 3 November 2026” date but a review date of December 2024, which has passed. Check GOV.UK for the current version.
2. **Scottish regulation title.** The FSS pages refer to the 2014 food information regulations without giving the Scottish instrument title. Confirm the exact reference on legislation.gov.uk before citing it formally.
3. **Emergency protocols** are demo or sample content (see section 9), not official guidance.
4. **Allergen matrix PDFs** in the project contain only the column headings, no completed dishes.
5. **Regulation (EU) 1169/2011** file is only a table of contents, so article wording was taken from the FSA technical guidance.
6. **Future-looking dates** in some sources (for example the egg guidance dated 21 August 2026) are treated as shown.
7. **Mixed jurisdiction materials.** MenuCal templates originated with the Food Safety Authority of Ireland and are used by FSS, so they are suitable in Scotland and workable in England, but the English regulator tools are the FSA ones.
8. **Northern Ireland** applies Regulation 1169/2011 directly rather than as assimilated law, and the FSA guidance covers NI, but this guide focuses on England and Scotland as requested.
