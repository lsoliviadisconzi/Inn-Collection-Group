Ripon
Length:
5 miles (8 km)

Moderate
This is one of the most scenic dog walks in the Ripon area, combining historic sights with beautiful Yorkshire countryside. The circular route takes you through Studley Royal’s parkland, past the elegant water gardens and towards the impressive ruins of Fountains Abbey.
You’ll also pass through the peaceful Seven Bridges Valley, following the River Skell through woodland and open countryside.
There are a few hills, steps and uneven sections, but nothing too strenuous, making this a great option for dogs who enjoy a longer countryside adventure. Dogs are welcome throughout the outdoor areas, although short leads are required.
National trust members get free entry.

A note before you set off:
These walks are intended as a helpful guide and starting point for exploring the local area with your dog. Routes, conditions and access can change, so please check current local guidance, weather conditions and access restrictions before setting off. Always use your own judgement and choose a route that’s suitable for you and your dog.
Tips for Walking Dogs in Yorkshire
Yorkshire’s countryside offers everything from peaceful woodland trails to rugged moorland hikes, but it’s important to keep your dog, local wildlife and other walkers in mind while exploring. To help you enjoy your walk safely and responsibly, remember to:
Keep dogs under control around livestock:
Yorkshire’s countryside is home to plenty of sheep, cattle and other livestock. Keep your dog on a lead when necessary and always follow local signage.
Check the route before setting off:
Some paths can become muddy, steep or difficult to navigate after wet weather, so choose a route that suits both your fitness levels.
Take plenty of water:
Don’t rely on streams or rivers for drinking water and carry enough for both you and your dog, particularly on longer walks.
Be prepared for changing weather:
Conditions can change quickly on the Yorkshire Dales and North York Moors, so check the forecast and pack suitable clothing.
Watch out for uneven terrain:
Rocky paths, stone steps, muddy tracks and limestone pavement can be challenging for dogs, particularly smaller breeds or those not used to longer walks.
Protect local wildlife:
Keep dogs on designated paths where required and avoid allowing them to chase birds, livestock or other wildlife.
Take dog waste home:
Always carry waste bags and dispose of them responsibly. If there are no bins nearby, take the waste home with you.
Keep your dog visible:
On open moorland or in areas with limited visibility, consider using a bright-coloured lead, harness or coat so you can keep track of your dog.
Give your dog a post-walk check:
After muddy or rural walks, check paws, ears and fur for grit, grass seeds, ticks or anything else they may have picked up along the way.
FAQs for your dog friendly walks
Can dogs swim in lakes, rivers and waterfalls in Yorkshire?
Some dogs can enjoy a paddle or swim in Yorkshire's rivers, lakes and other waterways, but you should always check the area first, as factors including rainfall can affect the safety of swimming in natural water. Fast-flowing rivers, waterfalls, deep water and slippery banks can be dangerous, while some locations may have restrictions on dogs entering the water. Avoid letting your dog swim near livestock, wildlife or areas with blue-green algae, and never encourage them into water that looks unsafe.

Are there off-lead dog walks in Yorkshire?
Yes, there are areas where dogs can enjoy some off-lead freedom in Yorkshire, but there aren't blanket rules covering every walking route. Always check local signs and landowner guidance before letting your dog off the lead. In areas with livestock, wildlife or busy paths, it's safest to keep your dog on a lead or under close control.

Where can I walk my dog near York city centre?
There are plenty of options for dog walks around York, including riverside routes along the River Ouse, the Knavesmire and green spaces such as Rowntree Park. For a longer countryside walk, head just outside the city to explore the surrounding villages, woodland and open countryside. Always check local signage for any seasonal restrictions and keep your dog under control around bust streets, any livestock en route, and other walkers.

What should I take on a long dog walk?
For a longer Yorkshire dog walk, pack plenty of fresh water, a collapsible bowl, dog waste bags, treats and a lead or harness. It's also useful to take a basic first-aid kit, a towel for muddy paws, your phone and a map or route downloaded for offline use. If you're heading into the Yorkshire Dales or North York Moors, check the weather beforehand and take extra layers for both you and your dog.

Are there stile-free dog walks in Yorkshire?
Yes, you can find stile-free dog walks across Yorkshire, although this will depend on the individual route. Riverside paths, parkland walks and accessible trails are often a good choice for avoiding stiles, with stiles being more common the further into the country you go, including the North York Moors. Before setting off, check the route details and look for walks described as stile-free or accessible, particularly if your dog struggles with steps, gates or climbing over stiles.

What are the best easy dog walks in Yorkshire?
Yorkshire has plenty of gentler routes for dogs who prefer a leisurely stroll. Easy options include riverside walks around Knaresborough, the paths around Fountains Abbey and Studley Royal, and shorter routes around May Beck and Falling Foss. Look for routes with relatively flat terrain, shorter distances and fewer steep or rocky sections if your dog isn't used to longer walks.

Where can I find dog walks with parking and toilets?
Many popular walking areas across Yorkshire have dedicated car parks and toilet facilities, particularly around visitor centres and popular attractions. Attractions including Fountains Abbey, Sutton Bank and Bolton Abbey, and towns including Helmsley and Knaresborough are good places to start when looking for walks with facilities nearby. It's worth checking parking arrangements and toilet opening times before setting off, particularly outside peak season.

Do dogs need to be on leads around livestock?
Dogs should be kept on a lead around livestock, particularly sheep and cattle, and always under close control in the countryside. Always follow signs and local restrictions.
