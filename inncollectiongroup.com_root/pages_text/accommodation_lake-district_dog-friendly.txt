Walks From Your Door in The Lake District
One of the best things about staying at our dog friendly inns is that some of the Lake District’s most spectacular walks are right on your doorstep. Whether your four-legged explorer prefers a gentle lakeside wander or a challenging fell hike, there’s a route to suit every pace (and every pair of paws).
Here are a few of our favourite dog-friendly walks, all within easy reach of our inns.
Aira Force
Distance:
1–2 miles (longer routes available)
Terrain:
Well-maintained woodland paths with some steps and uneven ground.
Best for:
Puppies, older dogs and families looking for a shorter adventure.
Lead or off lead?
Keep dogs on a lead around the waterfalls, bridges and busier sections of the trail.
Closest inn:
The Ullswater Inn
Aira Force is one of the Lake District’s most iconic waterfalls, winding through ancient woodland before tumbling into a dramatic gorge. It’s a beautiful walk in every season and perfect if you’re after stunning scenery without a full day’s hike.
Safety tip:
Paths and steps can become slippery after heavy rain, so take extra care around the waterfall.
Tarn Hows
Distance:
Around 2 miles circular
Terrain:
Wide, well-surfaced paths with gentle gradients.
Best for:
Older dogs, younger pups and relaxed family walks.
Lead or off lead?
Dogs can enjoy some off-lead time where appropriate, but keep them on a lead around grazing livestock.
Closest inn:
The Coniston Inn
Tarn Hows is one of the Lake District’s most picturesque beauty spots. Expect peaceful woodland, stunning views and an easy-going route that’s ideal for dogs of all ages.
Safety tip:
Be mindful of sheep grazing nearby, particularly during lambing season.
Orrest Head
Distance:
Around 2.5 miles return
Terrain:
Woodland paths and moderate uphill sections.
Best for:
Dogs with moderate fitness looking for a rewarding climb.
Lead or off lead?
Off-lead is suitable in quieter woodland areas where safe, but leads are recommended near roads and busy viewpoints.
Closest inn:
The Waterhead Inn
and
The Wateredge Inn
A favourite for first-time visitors, Orrest Head offers one of the best panoramic views over Lake Windermere without requiring a full day’s hike. It’s a fantastic choice for sunrise or sunset walks with your canine companion.
Safety tip:
Popular viewpoints can get busy, so keep your dog close when other walkers are around.
Loughrigg Fell
Distance:
Around 4 miles circular
Terrain:
Well-marked paths with gentle climbs, rocky sections and open fell.
Best for:
Dogs with moderate fitness and owners looking for a shorter fell walk.
Lead or off lead?
Dogs can enjoy off-lead sections where appropriate, but should be kept under close control near grazing sheep and other livestock.
Closest inn:
The Swan at Grasmere
Loughrigg Fell is one of the best dog-friendly walks near Grasmere, combining manageable climbs with some of the finest views in the Lake District. Along the way, you’ll discover peaceful woodland, open fell and panoramic vistas across Grasmere, Rydal Water and Windermere. It’s the perfect choice if you’re after a rewarding walk without committing to a full-day hike.
Safety tip:
The rocky sections can become slippery after wet weather, so take extra care and keep your dog close on steeper descents.
Old Man of Coniston
Distance:
Around 8 miles circular
Terrain:
Rocky fell paths with steep ascents and uneven ground.
Best for:
Hill-fit dogs who enjoy a longer adventure.
Lead or off lead?
Dogs can enjoy off-lead sections where it’s safe to do so, but should be kept on a lead around livestock and on busier stretches of the route.
Closest inn:
The Coniston Inn
If you and your dog are looking for a challenge, the Old Man of Coniston is one of the Lake District’s most rewarding walks. Climb through historic slate quarry landscapes before reaching the summit, where you’ll be treated to spectacular views across Coniston Water and the surrounding fells. It’s a classic Lake District hike that’s well worth the effort.
Safety tip:
Check the weather before setting off, as conditions can change quickly on the higher fells. Pack plenty of water for both you and your dog, especially during warmer months.
Dodd Wood and Dodd Summit
Distance:
Around 4.5 miles circular
Terrain:
Woodland trails, forest tracks and a steady uphill climb to the summit.
Best for:
Dogs with moderate fitness who enjoy a mix of woodland and fell walking.
Lead or off lead?
Dogs can enjoy stretches off lead where safe, but should be kept on a lead around livestock, wildlife and busier sections of the trail.
Closest inn:
The Pheasant Inn
Just a short drive from Bassenthwaite, Dodd Wood offers one of the Lake District’s most rewarding woodland walks. Wander through peaceful forest trails before climbing to Dodd Summit, where you’ll be rewarded with sweeping views across Bassenthwaite Lake, Derwentwater and the northern fells. Keep your eyes peeled along the way – you may even spot ospreys soaring overhead during the nesting season.
Whether you’re after a gentle stroll beneath the trees or a more energetic climb to the top, Dodd Wood is a fantastic choice for dogs and their owners alike.
Safety tip:
Be aware of cyclists and forestry vehicles on shared tracks, and keep your dog close if you encounter wildlife or nesting birds.
Dog-Safe Lake Swimming
Many dogs love cooling off in the Lake District’s lakes and tarns, especially during warmer weather. Always choose calm, accessible spots and check the water before letting your dog swim. Avoid areas with strong currents, boat traffic or steep banks.
It’s also worth watching out for blue-green algae, which can sometimes appear during warmer months and is highly toxic to dogs. If you notice green scum or warning signs around the water, keep your dog well away and choose another spot for a paddle.
Before setting off, it’s a good idea to read the Lake District National Park’s
guidance on rules for dogs in the National Park
, including advice on livestock, wildlife and responsible walking with your dog.
