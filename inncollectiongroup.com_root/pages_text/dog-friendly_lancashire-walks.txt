Step into Lancashire’s own little slice of Middle-earth on the Tolkien Trail. Starting in Hurst Green, the route winds through the beautiful Ribble Valley around Stonyhurst and along the River Ribble, with woodland, open countryside and historic buildings along the way.

Derwent Walk
Location:
Scorton, Forest of Bowland
Length:
3.6 miles (5.8 km)
Difficulty:
Moderate
Nicky Nook packs a surprising amount into a relatively short walk. From Scorton, the route heads through countryside and woodland before climbing towards the top of Nicky Nook, where you’ll be rewarded with sweeping views over the Fylde Coast, Morecambe Bay and towards the Lake District on a clear day.
The route can feel much wilder than its distance suggests, with some uneven and boggy sections after wet weather. It’s a great choice for energetic dogs who enjoy a bit of a climb. Nicky Nook is also a recognised Forest of Bowland walking route, with local walking guidance recommending dogs on leads.

Ward's Stone from Tarnbrook
Location:
Tarnbrook, Forest of Bowland
Length:
8 miles (12.9 km)
Difficulty:
Challenging
This is the big one for experienced walkers and seriously energetic dogs. The route towards Ward’s Stone takes you deep into the wilder side of the Forest of Bowland, with open moorland, rugged ground and huge views across the surrounding fells.
It’s a much more demanding day out than the shorter valley walks, with rough and potentially boggy sections that can become particularly challenging after wet weather. There’s little in the way of facilities once you’re out on the moors, so pack plenty of water and snacks for both of you.
Ward’s Stone is Lancashire’s second-highest hill and routes to it are best suited to fit walkers and dogs accustomed to longer fell days.

A note before you set off:
These walks are intended as a helpful guide and starting point for exploring the local area with your dog. Routes, conditions and access can change, so please check current local guidance, weather conditions and access restrictions before setting off. Always use your own judgement and choose a route that’s suitable for you and your dog.
Tips for Walking Dogs in Lancashire
Whether you’re heading for the coast, exploring a country park or enjoying a woodland wander, Lancashire has plenty of great places to stretch four legs. Keep these tips in mind for a happy and safe walk for everyone:
Choose a suitable route:
Lancashire has everything from flat coastal paths to steep fell walks, so choose a route that suits your dog’s fitness and experience.
Keep your dog under control around livestock and wildlife:
Sheep, cattle and other animals are common across Lancashire’s countryside, and The Forest of Bowland and nature reserves are home to lots of wildlife. Keep dogs close and follow any local restrictions, particularly during nesting seasons.
Take extra care on coastal walks:
Check the tide times before heading out around Morecambe Bay and never attempt to cross tidal sands without someone experienced with you.
Pack water for both of you:
Some longer countryside and fell routes have few facilities, so take enough fresh water for your dog, particularly on warmer days.
Expect muddy paws:
Woodland, moorland and riverside paths can become very muddy after wet weather. A towel in the car is always a good idea!
Watch out for uneven ground:
Rocky paths, steep sections, tree roots and slippery surfaces can all feature on Lancashire’s more challenging walks, so take your time.
Take your rubbish:
Take poo bags with you and always take them home or use the bins provided. Avoid leaving bags hanging on fences or gates.
Be considerate of other walkers:
Keep dogs close on narrow paths and around cyclists, runners, families and horse riders, giving others plenty of space to pass.
Check local signs:
Dog restrictions can vary between beaches, nature reserves, parks and access land, so always follow the latest signage when you arrive.
Make sure your dog has a safe place to rest afterwards:
After a muddy adventure, there’s nothing better than a dog-friendly pub, a hearty meal and somewhere cosy to put your paws up.
FAQs for your dog friendly walks
Where can I walk my dog near Blackpool?
There are plenty of places to enjoy a dog walk around Blackpool, from coastal promenades and beaches to parks and nature reserves. The Fylde Coast offers lovely seaside walking, while nearby countryside provides quieter routes away from the hustle and bustle. Just remember to check local restrictions before setting off, as some areas have seasonal dog restrictions and lead requirements.

Are dogs allowed on beaches in Lancashire?
Dogs are welcome on many beaches and coastal areas in Lancashire, but restrictions vary between locations and can be seasonal. For example, in Blackpool, dogs are not permitted on the beach between North Pier and the Mirror Ball from 1 May to 30 September, while designated stretches north and south of this area welcome dogs all year. Always check local signage before letting your dog onto the sand.

Are there off-lead dog walks in Lancashire?
Yes, there are places where dogs can be walked off lead in Lancashire, provided they are under effective control and there are no local restrictions. However, dogs should be kept on a lead around livestock, and on open access land there are additional legal requirements, including between 1 March and 31 July to protect ground-nesting birds. Always follow signs and local guidance for the area you're visiting.

What are the best easy dog walks in Lancashire?
Lancashire has plenty of easy walks for dogs who prefer a gentler pace. Flat coastal routes, riverside paths and country parks are great choices, with options such as Lytham Green, Morecambe Promenade, Fleetwood and Beacon Fell offering different scenery without demanding climbs. For a little more variety, Wycoller Country Park has woodland and farmland paths, as well as plenty of space to explore

Are there stile-free dog walks in Lancashire?
Yes, although not every countryside route will be stile-free. For an easier walk with fewer obstacles, look for promenades, surfaced paths and country parks. Coastal routes and some of Lancashire's parks can be particularly suitable if you're walking with a smaller, older or less mobile dog. Always check the route details before setting off, as Lancashire's public rights of way can include uneven, steep or narrow sections.

Where can I find dog walks with parking and toilets?
Country parks are a good place to start if you're looking for a dog walk with useful facilities nearby. Beacon Fell Country Park has parking, toilets, picnic areas and a café, while Wycoller Country Park also has car parks, toilets and picnic facilities. Other Lancashire countryside sites parking facilities are listed on our guide, however toilet facilities can be limited if opting for a more rural route.

What should I take on a long dog walk?
For a longer Lancashire walk, pack plenty of water for you and your dog, some treats or food, poo bags, a lead and a suitable dog harness. A towel is handy for muddy paws, while waterproofs, a map or phone with GPS and a portable bowl are useful for longer countryside or fell routes. Check the forecast before you leave and be prepared for conditions to change, particularly on exposed routes.
Close
Do dogs need to be on leads around livestock?
It's good practice to keep your dog on a lead around livestock, and there are situations where it is a legal requirement. On open access land, dogs must be kept on a lead no more than two metres long around livestock, while between 1 March and 31 July they must also be on a lead on open access land to protect ground-nesting birds.
