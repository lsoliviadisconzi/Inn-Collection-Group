Find the Perfect Dog Walk Near You in County Durham
Finding a great dog walk is all about matching the route to you and your four-legged friend. From short, easy strolls for a relaxed wander to longer trails for dogs who love to clock up the miles, Co. Durham has plenty of options to choose from.
We’ve rounded up some of our favourite dog-friendly walks across the county, with different lengths, terrains and difficulty levels to help you find the right fit. So, grab the lead, pop a few treats in your pocket and get ready to explore some of Durham’s best countryside, coast and woodland together.

Lead guidance:

What to expect:

There are some steps, uneven sections and paths close to the cliff edge, so keep an eye on your pup and take your time. After wet weather, some sections can also be muddy.

Tips for Walking Dogs in County Durham
To help everyone enjoy County Durham’s countryside, coast and woodland, keep these handy tips in mind:
Keep dogs on a lead around livestock, wildlife and busy areas.
Carry plenty of fresh water, particularly for longer walks and warmer days.
Bring dog waste bags and always pick up after your dog.
Check the weather before setting off, especially if your route takes you onto higher or more exposed ground.
Be prepared for muddy, uneven or slippery paths after wet weather.
Take extra care around rivers, reservoirs, waterfalls and coastal cliff edges.
Consider your dog’s fitness and experience before tackling longer or more challenging routes.
County Durham has plenty of working farmland, so keep dogs under close control around livestock and follow local signs and restrictions.
Be mindful of other walkers, cyclists and horse riders, particularly on shared trails such as those in Hamsterley Forest.
Before setting off, check any local access restrictions or seasonal guidance for the area you’re visiting.
FAQs for your dog friendly walks
Are there off-lead dog walks in Durham?
There are areas in County Durham where dogs can enjoy freedom, but there isn't a blanket rule that dogs can be off lead. Always check local signs and make sure your dog is under control. Around livestock and wildlife, dogs should be kept on a lead, while some places such as the Durham Coast ask owners to keep dogs on a short lead in sensitive areas and near cliff edges. Always use your judgment to make sure everyone stays safe!

There are plenty of dog-friendly walks close to Durham city centre. The River Wear walk is a great option, taking you through peaceful riverside paths and green spaces with views towards Durham Cathedral. You can also head to Low Burnhall, just south of the city, for woodland trails and open countryside, which is particularly handy if you're looking for a quieter walk away from the city centre.

Can dogs swim in lakes, rivers or waterfalls in County Durham?
Some dogs may enjoy paddling or swimming in suitable areas of County Durham's rivers and waterways, but you should always check the conditions first. Avoid letting your dog swim in fast-flowing water, near waterfalls or anywhere with strong currents, steep banks or deep drops. Reservoirs and other water bodies can also have hidden hazards, so look for local signs and use designated areas where available. When in doubt, a shallow stream or safe paddling spot is a much better choice.

Are there stile-free dog walks in Durham?
Yes, there are several dog-friendly walks in County Durham with fewer obstacles, although this can vary depending on the route you choose. Riverside paths, woodland trails and some of the shorter routes around Durham City are generally good choices if you're looking to avoid stiles. It's worth checking the specific route details before setting off, and use your judgement on choosing a walk if you are aware your dog has mobility issues or struggles with gates and steps.

What are the best easy dog walks in Durham?
For an easy dog-friendly walk, the Durham City River Walk, Low Burnhall Woodland and shorter routes around Raby Castle's deer park are good options. These offer a mix of riverside, woodland and parkland scenery without the steep climbs or long distances of some of Durham's more challenging countryside routes. Always check the terrain and any local restrictions before setting off.

Do dogs need to be on leads around livestock?
Yes, you should keep your dog on a lead and under close control whenever you're walking near livestock. This is particularly important around sheep and cattle, as dogs can worry or chase livestock. Always follow local signage and give animals plenty of space.

What should I take on a long dog walk?
For a longer walk in County Durham, take plenty of fresh water for both you and your dog, a collapsible bowl, leads and poo bags. It's also worth packing treats, a small first-aid kit, your phone, a towel for muddy paws and a waterproof layer in case the weather changes. For longer or more remote routes, make sure you have an appropriate map or offline route downloaded and let someone know where you're going.
