Find the Perfect Dog Walk Near You in Tyne and Wear
Tyne and Wear is full of opportunities to get out and explore with your dog, whether you fancy relaxed seaside dog walks or a longer route to really stretch your legs. Follow the coastline around Tynemouth, Cullercoats and St Mary’s Island, wander through leafy woodland or discover open spaces and country parks around Newcastle, Gateshead and Sunderland. Whatever your dog’s idea of a good walk, there’s a route to suit.
Planning a dog-friendly break in Tyne and Wear? Our guide brings together some of the best walks across the region, with a mix of coastal scenery, riverside paths, woodland trails and green spaces. From the buzz of the coast to quieter countryside escapes, you can discover a different side of Tyne and Wear with your four-legged companion by your side.
To help you choose your perfect route, we’ve included all the useful details you need before setting off, including distance, difficulty, terrain, parking, public transport and guidance on keeping your dog on a lead. All that’s left to do is grab the treats, clip on the lead and set off exploring.

This is a lovely introduction to walking in Newcastle, taking you through the leafy valley of Jesmond Dene and into Armstrong Park. Follow the Ouseburn through woodland, passing waterfalls, historic buildings and plenty of places for your dog to stop and sniff.
The route is generally gentle underfoot, although some of the smaller paths can become muddy after rain. With cafés along the way, it's an easy one to turn into a relaxed morning or afternoon out with your four-legged friend.

If you and your dog are looking for a walk combining history, sea views and a stretch of golden sand, look no further than the coastal town of Tynemouth. Make sure to check for seasonal dog restrictions on the southern section of Longsands, which come into place during summer months, where the route along the promenade can be taken before heading onto Longsands North.
There's plenty to keep curious dogs entertained, from sandy stretches and grassy clifftops to the sights and sounds of the North Sea. The route is relatively easy, although take extra care around cliff edges and keep your dog close in busier areas.

Promenade, paved coastal paths, and some grassy sections

St Mary's Island Car Park
Public transport:
Buses serve the Whitley Bay area. Tynemouth Metro Station is close to the finish, making it straightforward to return by public transport.
Lead guidance:
Keep dogs under close control along the coastal path, and take note of seasonal beach restrictions if walking on the beaches.

The Tynemouth Castle Inn

Starting at the iconic St Mary’s Lighthouse, head south along the coastline towards Whitley Bay, following the seafront past the sweeping sandy beach and Spanish City before continuing towards Cullercoats. From here, the route follows the coast around Cullercoats Bay and along the clifftops above Tynemouth Longsands, eventually arriving at the dramatic headland of Tynemouth Priory and Castle.

Make sure to check the tide times for St Mary's Island, with the causeway being submerged at high tide!

Tips for Walking Dogs in Tyne and Wear
Whether you’re heading for the coast, exploring a country park or enjoying a woodland wander, Tyne and Wear has plenty of great places to stretch four legs. Keep these tips in mind for a happy and hassle-free walk:
Check local dog restrictions:
Beach and park rules can vary, particularly during the summer. For example, parts of the North Tyneside coastline have seasonal dog restrictions, while Sunderland has dog exclusion zones at sections of Roker and Seaburn beaches from 1 May to 30 September.
Keep your dog under control:
Make sure your dog is in sight and comes back reliably when called. Keep them on a lead where signs ask you to, and around livestock, wildlife, roads and cliff edges.
Be extra careful around livestock:
Tyne and Wear’s countryside routes can pass through farmland, so always give livestock plenty of space and put your dog on a lead when needed. Never allow your dog to chase or worry animals.
Take care along the coast:
Coastal walks around Tynemouth, Cullercoats, Whitley Bay and Sunderland offer spectacular views, but cliff edges, changing weather and uneven ground mean it’s important to keep dogs close.
Check the tide:
If your walk takes you near St Mary’s Lighthouse or other tidal areas, check tide times before setting off. Never let your dog cross a tidal causeway if conditions aren’t safe.
Pack water and a bowl:
Even on shorter walks, fresh water is handy, particularly on sunny days when you’re spending time on exposed coastal paths.
Bring a lead even if your dog usually walks off lead:
You never know when you’ll encounter livestock, horses, cyclists, wildlife or a section where local rules require dogs to be leashed.
Bag it and bin it:
Always pick up after your dog and take waste with you if there isn’t a suitable bin nearby. Dog waste can be harmful to people, livestock and wildlife.
Stick to the path:
Following marked routes helps protect wildlife, crops and the surrounding countryside. It also makes it easier to keep your dog away from livestock and private land.
Be prepared for muddy paws:
Woodland and countryside routes can get muddy after wet weather, so towels, water and a boot-cleaning routine can save your car (and your accommodation!) from a muddy makeover.
Give wildlife plenty of space:
Coastal and countryside walks are home to birds and other wildlife. Keep dogs from chasing or disturbing animals and follow any local signage, particularly around sensitive coastal habitats.
FAQs for your dog friendly walks
Where can I walk my dog near Newcastle?
There are plenty of dog-friendly walks close to Newcastle, from leafy Jesmond Dene and riverside paths at Newburn to longer trails through Derwent Walk Country Park. If you fancy a trip to the coast, Tynemouth, Cullercoats and Whitley Bay offer scenic seaside walks with plenty of fresh sea air.

Are dogs allowed on beaches in Tyne and Wear?
Yes, dogs are allowed on many beaches in Tyne and Wear, although restrictions apply to certain areas at particular times of year. In North Tyneside, seasonal dog restrictions run from
1 May to 30 September
on parts of Whitley Bay, Cullercoats Bay, King Edward's Bay and southern Tynemouth Longsands. Sunderland also has seasonal exclusion zones at parts of Roker and Seaburn beaches. Always check local signs before letting your dog onto the sand.

Are there off-lead dog walks in Tyne and Wear?
Dogs can be walked off lead in some areas of Tyne and Wear, provided they are under effective control and local signs don't require them to be on a lead. Around livestock, wildlife, roads, busy areas and cliff edges, it's best to keep your dog on a lead. Always check signage at your chosen walking spot, as local restrictions can apply.
Close
Can dogs swim in the sea on Tyne and Wear coast?
Dogs can enjoy a paddle or swim in some areas of the Tyne and Wear coastline, but you should always check local restrictions and assess the conditions first. Be particularly careful around strong currents, tides, deep water, slippery rocks and steep riverbanks. Around the coast, never let your dog enter the water if conditions look unsafe, and keep them away from areas with dangerous currents or exposed cliffs.

What are the best easy dog walks in Tyne and Wear?
For an easy-going dog walk, try Jesmond Dene, Saltwell Park, Watergate Forest Park or the coastal paths around Tynemouth and Cullercoats. These routes offer relatively gentle terrain and a mixture of woodland, parks, riverside paths and seaside scenery, making them ideal for a relaxed stroll with your dog.

Are there stile-free dog walks in Tyne and Wear?
Yes. Many of Tyne and Wear's coastal and urban park walks are largely stile-free, making them particularly convenient if you're looking for an easier route with your dog. Promenade and coastal routes around Tynemouth, Cullercoats and Whitley Bay, as well as walks through parks such as Saltwell Park, offer mostly straightforward paths.

Where can I find dog walks with parking and toilets?
Several popular walking spots in Tyne and Wear have nearby car parks and facilities, including Jesmond Dene, Rising Sun Country Park, Herrington Country Park, Souter Lighthouse and Gibside. It's worth checking the facilities before setting off, particularly if you're planning a longer walk, as toilets and opening hours can vary.

What should I take on a long dog walk?
For a longer walk, pack fresh water and a collapsible bowl, dog poo bags, treats, a lead and spare towel. A long line can also be useful if you're walking somewhere where your dog needs more freedom but must remain under control. For coastal or countryside routes, check the weather and tide times beforehand and take a fully charged phone, suitable footwear and a basic first-aid kit.
