Find the Perfect Dog Walk Near You in North Wales
Ready to get out and about with your four-legged explorer? North Wales is packed with places to stretch your legs, from peaceful riverside routes and ancient forests to rugged peaks and sweeping shores. We’ve rounded up some of the best dog-friendly walks across the region, so you can find a route that suits both you and your pup – whether that’s a leisurely leg-stretcher or a more challenging countryside adventure.

Betws-y-Coed Bridges
Location:
Betws-y-Coed, Conwy
Length:
2.2 miles (3.6 km)
Route type:
Circular
Difficulty:
Easy
Terrain:
Well-formed paths, woodland tracks, riverside paths and some gently undulating sections
Starting point:
LL24 0AH
Closest car park:
Cae Llan Car Park
Public transport:
Betws-y-Coed has both train and bus connections
Lead guidance:
Recommended for dogs to stay under close control around livestock and other walkers
Closest inn:
The Swallow Falls Inn
What to expect:
Take a gentle wander through the pretty village of Betws-y-Coed, known as the “Gateway to Snowdonia”, before heading into the surrounding woodland. The route follows sections of the Llugwy River and crosses some of the village’s historic bridges, with plenty of trees, streams and scenic spots to investigate.
It’s a lovely option for a relaxed stroll, particularly if you’re looking for a shorter walk that still gives you a taste of the beautiful North Wales countryside.

2.8 miles (4.5 km)

Out and back

Easy
Terrain:
Wide, well-formed paths with some uneven and rough sections and a steady climb
Starting point:
LL33 0LP
Closest car park:
Aber Falls Lower Car Park

Buses run to Abergwyngregyn from Bangor or Llanfairfechan
Lead guidance:

Follow the path through woodland and open countryside towards one of North Wales’ most impressive waterfalls. The route gradually climbs alongside the river, with the sound of rushing water accompanying you along the way, before opening out to reveal the spectacular Aber Falls.
There are plenty of natural sights and smells to keep curious dogs entertained, as well as opportunities to stop and take in the scenery. A great choice for a scenic walk without committing to a full-day mountain hike.

Great Orme Nature Trail
Location:
Llandudno
Length:
3.5 miles (5.5 km)
Route type:
Circular
Difficulty:
Easy to Moderate
Terrain:
Grassy paths, tracks and roads, with some steep sections
Starting point:
LL30 2XG
Closest car park:
Great Orme Summit Pay & Display Car Park
Public transport:
Llandudno railway station, local buses and the Great Orme Tramway
Lead guidance:
Lead recommended as sheep and goats graze freely on the headland
Closest inn:
The Bull's Head Inn
What to expect:
Enjoy panoramic views across Llandudno, the North Wales coast and out towards the Irish Sea as you explore the dramatic landscape of the Great Orme. The route winds across grassy paths and tracks, passing through the Orme’s distinctive limestone landscape and areas where you may spot the resident wildlife.
Keep your eyes peeled for the famous Kashmiri goats, as well as seabirds and other wildlife along the way., but make sure dogs are kept on a lead when passing livestock sections. With a mixture of gentle stretches and steeper sections, it’s a varied walk with plenty to see and sniff.

Location:
Dolgellau to Barmouth
Length:
9 miles (15 km)
Route type:
Linear
Difficulty:
Easy to moderate
Terrain:
Flat, wide and mostly even trail with no steep sections
Starting point
: LL40 1DL
Closest car park:
Y Marian Car Park
Public transport:
Bus services connect Dolgellau and Barmouth, with railway access is available at Barmouth and nearby stations
Lead guidance:
Recommended due to livestock and busy public sections
Closest inn:
The Swallow Falls Inn
What to expect:
If your dog loves a long walk, the Mawddach Trail is a fantastic choice. Following the southern shore of the spectacular Mawddach Estuary, this mostly level trail offers beautiful views across the water towards the surrounding mountains.
The wide, relatively even path makes it an enjoyable option for a leisurely stroll, jog or bike ride, with plenty of scenery to keep you company along the way. You don’t have to tackle the entire 15km either – you can join the trail at different points and tailor the length to suit you and your pup.

Holyhead, Anglesey

3 miles (5 km)
Route type:
Circular
Difficulty:
Moderate
Terrain:

South Stack RSPB Car Park

There are steps and uneven sections along the route, as well as exposed cliff edges, so keep your pup close and take care around the coastline. It’s a brilliant choice for dogs who love exploring somewhere new and owners who enjoy big sea views.

Newborough, Anglesey
Length:
5 miles (8 km)
Route type:
Circular
Difficulty:
Easy to moderate
Terrain:
Forest tracks, sandy paths, beach and uneven coastal ground
Starting point
: LL61 6SG
Closest car park:
Newborough Beach Car Park
Public transport:
Bus services operate between Llangefni and Newborough, with connections to Bangor and other parts of Anglesey
Lead guidance:
Dogs must be kept under close control. Check seasonal restrictions before visiting the beach and Llanddwyn Island.

What to expect:
Combine woodland, beach and beautiful coastal scenery on this varied walk through Newborough Forest towards Llanddwyn Island. The route winds beneath Corsican pines before opening out onto the sandy beach, with views across the Menai Strait towards the mountains of Eryri.
Continue towards romantic Llanddwyn Island, famous for its lighthouse, ruined church and connection with the Welsh patron saint of lovers, Santes Dwynwen.
With plenty of space to explore and lots of different terrain underfoot, this is a fantastic choice for dogs who love a varied day out.

Tips for Walking Dogs in North Wales
To help you and your four-legged friend make the most of North Wales’ beautiful coast, countryside and mountains, keep these tips in mind:
Keep dogs on a lead around livestock and wildlife, and always follow local signage. Be mindful that much of the North Wales countryside is working farmland.
Carry plenty of fresh water for both you and your dog, particularly on longer walks and warmer days.
Bring dog waste bags and take all waste home with you if there are no bins available.
Check the weather forecast before heading into Eryri (Snowdonia) or onto higher ground, as conditions can change quickly s you reach higher summits.
Take extra care on rocky, muddy or uneven paths, particularly after wet weather when surfaces can become slippery.
Make sure the length and difficulty of your chosen route is suitable for your dog’s fitness and experience.
Take extra care around rivers, lakes, waterfalls and cliff edges, particularly with dogs who like to explore off the lead.
If you’re visiting the coast, check local and seasonal dog restrictions before letting your pup enjoy the beach.
Before setting off, check the latest guidance from
Eryri National Park
for information on responsible dog walking, access restrictions, livestock and wildlife.
FAQs for your dog friendly walks
Where can I walk my dog in Snowdonia?
Snowdonia, now known as Eryri National Park, has plenty of fantastic places to explore with your dog. From gentle walks around Llyn Padarn and through Betws-y-Coed to more challenging mountain routes around Beddgelert and the Ogwen Valley, there are walks to suit a range of abilities. Just be sure to choose a route that suits your dog's fitness and keep them under close control around livestock and wildlife.

What are the best easy dog walks in North Wales?
For a gentler adventure, try the Betws-y-Coed Bridges walk, Aber Falls, sections of the Mawddach Trail or a stroll around Llyn Padarn. These routes offer beautiful scenery without requiring a full mountain climb, making them great options for a more leisurely walk with your dog.

Do dogs need to be on leads around livestock?
Yes. Whenever you're near sheep, cattle or other livestock, it's best to put your dog on a short lead and keep them close by. In Eryri (Snowdonia), dogs must also be on a lead on open access land between 1 March and 31 July, even if there is no livestock present, to help protect young animals and ground-nesting birds. Always follow local signage and keep your dog under effective control.

Can dogs swim in lakes or rivers in Snowdonia and North Wales?
Dogs can enjoy paddling or swimming in some lakes and rivers, but it’s important to check local signage and access rules first. Be aware of strong currents, steep banks, deep water and blue-green algae, which can be harmful to dogs. Never encourage your dog to enter water if you’re unsure of the conditions, and always supervise them closely.

Are there stile-free dog walks in North Wales?
Yes, there are several routes in North Wales that are suitable for walkers looking to avoid stiles. The Mawddach Trail is a particularly good option, with a wide, mostly level path, while some accessible lakeside and former railway routes also offer easier access. Route conditions can change, so check the latest accessibility information before setting off.

Are there off-lead dog walks in North Wales?
There are plenty of open spaces in North Wales where dogs may be able to enjoy some off-lead time, but this will depend on the location, local restrictions and what is happening around you. Always keep your dog under close control, and use a lead around livestock, wildlife, busy paths and roads. Be sure to check local signage too, as some areas have seasonal restrictions or specific rules for dogs.

Are dogs allowed on beaches in North Wales?
Yes, dogs are welcome on many beaches across North Wales, making the region a great choice for a seaside walk with your four-legged friend. Some beaches have seasonal restrictions or designated dog-friendly areas, particularly during the summer months, so it’s always best to check local rules and follow signage.

What should I take on a long dog walk?
For a longer adventure, pack plenty of water for both you and your dog, a collapsible bowl, dog waste bags, some treats or food and a lead. Depending on the route and weather, you may also want a towel, tick remover, basic first-aid supplies and an extra layer for yourself. Before heading into the mountains, check the weather and make sure the route is suitable for both your fitness levels.
