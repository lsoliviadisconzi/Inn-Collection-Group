Stock Ghyll Force
Location:
Ambleside
Length:
2 miles (3.2 km)
Difficulty:
Easy
Stock Ghyll Force is one of the most accessible waterfalls in the Lake District and makes an excellent short walk for dogs. Beginning in the centre of Ambleside, the route quickly enters peaceful woodland before reaching the impressive 70-foot waterfall.
The trail follows well-maintained woodland paths with plenty of shade, making it ideal during warmer weather. Dogs will enjoy exploring the woodland scents and can often paddle in shallow streams along the route.
Although there are some stone steps and uneven sections near the viewing platform, the walk is manageable for most abilities. After your walk, Ambleside offers numerous dog-friendly cafés and pubs where both you and your dog can relax.

Bowness-on-Windermere
Length:
3 miles (4.8 km)
Difficulty:
Easy to Moderate
Brant Fell offers one of the best viewpoints over Lake Windermere without requiring a strenuous climb. This circular route climbs steadily through woodland before opening onto grassy fell tops with panoramic views across England’s largest lake.
The paths are generally quiet, making it a peaceful choice for dogs that prefer less crowded routes. There are plenty of open areas where dogs can enjoy stretching their legs, although they should remain under control around grazing sheep.
The walk is relatively short but rewarding, making it ideal for families or visitors looking for a gentle introduction to Lakeland fell walking.

For a relaxing lakeside adventure, the Coniston Lakeshore Path is hard to beat. Following the shores of Coniston Water, this mostly level route provides uninterrupted lake views with beautiful woodland sections and plenty of places to stop.
Dogs will love exploring the shoreline, and there are several safe spots where they can enjoy a paddle. The relatively flat terrain makes this walk suitable for older dogs, puppies and less experienced walkers.
With stunning mountain backdrops throughout, it’s an excellent choice for photographers and anyone wanting a leisurely day outdoors.

The Grasmere Lake Circuit is one of the most picturesque easy walks in the Lake District. The circular route follows the shoreline of Grasmere with beautiful views across the water towards Helm Crag and the surrounding hills.
The path alternates between woodland and open lakeside sections, offering plenty of variety for both dogs and owners. There are numerous spots where dogs can safely paddle, while benches along the route provide ideal places to enjoy the scenery.

One of the Lake District’s most famous waterfalls, Aira Force offers an enchanting woodland walk suitable for most visitors. The circular route winds through ancient woodland before reaching the dramatic waterfall, where water plunges over a series of rocky cascades.
Dogs will enjoy the shaded woodland environment and the sounds of rushing water throughout the walk. There are several flights of steps, so take extra care if conditions are wet.
The route can become busy during weekends and school holidays, so visiting earlier in the day is recommended if your dog prefers quieter surroundings.

The Coffin Route
Location:
Ambleside to Grasmere
Length:
5 miles (8 km)
Difficulty:
Moderate
The historic Coffin Route links Ambleside and Grasmere along one of the Lake District’s most scenic low-level walks. Once used to transport coffins between villages, today it offers peaceful countryside, beautiful woodland and spectacular fell views.
The trail passes Rydal Hall, crosses charming stone bridges and winds beneath impressive Lakeland peaks without requiring any difficult climbing.
Dogs will appreciate the varied terrain and quieter atmosphere compared to some of the more popular routes. There are occasional livestock fields, so keeping dogs under close control is essential.

Old Man of Coniston
Location:
Coniston
Length:
7 miles (11.3 km)
Difficulty:
Challenging
For a proper Lakeland adventure, it doesn’t get much better than the Old Man of Coniston. Rising dramatically above Coniston village, this iconic 803m fell combines spectacular mountain scenery with a fascinating industrial past.
The route climbs through the old slate quarry workings, passing remnants of buildings and machinery before reaching the beautiful mountain tarn of Low Water. From here, the path becomes steeper and rougher as it heads towards the summit, where you’ll be rewarded with sweeping views across Coniston Water and the surrounding fells
It’s a fantastic walk for energetic dogs who are comfortable with longer distances, steep climbs and rocky terrain. However, this isn’t one for every four-legged explorer. Dogs should be confident on uneven ground and owners should take extra care on steeper sections, particularly in poor weather.

Fairfield Horseshoe
Location:
Ambleside to Grasmere
Length:
10 miles (16.1 km)
Difficulty:
Challenging
If you’re looking for a full-day challenge with plenty of summits along the way, the Fairfield Horseshoe is a fantastic choice. Starting in Ambleside, this classic circular route takes in a series of peaks surrounding the beautiful Rydal and Fairfield valleys.
The route climbs steadily out of Ambleside before heading higher into the fells. You’ll tackle several summits before reaching Fairfield itself, the highest point of the horseshoe at 873m.
It’s a long and demanding hike requiring the full day, but the varied scenery makes every section feel different. Dogs who enjoy long-distance fell walking will love the open landscapes, grassy paths and mountain terrain, although there are rocky and steep sections where extra care is needed.

A note before you set off:
These walks are intended as a helpful guide and starting point for exploring the local area with your dog. Routes, conditions and access can change, so please check current local guidance, weather conditions and access restrictions before setting off. Always use your own judgement and choose a route that’s suitable for you and your dog.
Tips for Walking Dogs in the Lake District
To help everyone enjoy these beautiful landscapes, remember to:
Keep dogs on a lead around livestock and wildlife.
Carry plenty of water, particularly during warmer months.
Bring dog waste bags and use bins where available.
Check weather conditions before heading onto higher ground.
Be aware that rocky paths can become slippery after rain.
Consider your dog’s fitness before attempting longer fell walks.
Please remember that many Lake District fells are working farmland. Keep dogs under close control around livestock and follow local signage, particularly during lambing season.
Before setting off, it’s a good idea to read the Lake District National Park’s
guidance on rules for dogs in the National Park
, including advice on livestock, wildlife and responsible walking with your dog.
FAQs for your dog friendly walks
Are there off-lead dog walks in the Lake District?
There are areas across the Lake District where dogs can enjoy some freedom, but there aren't blanket off-lead areas across the region. Many walking routes pass through farmland or areas with livestock, so dogs should be kept on a lead when required and always under close control. Look out for local signage and carry a lead with you, even if you plan to let your dog off in suitable areas.

Are there stile-free dog walks in the Lake District?
Yes, there are plenty of dog walks in the Lake District that avoid stiles, although it's worth checking the route before setting off. Lakeside paths, woodland trails and some low-level routes tend to be better options for dogs who struggle with stiles or gates. Look for clearly marked paths and check the route details for any accessibility information before you go.
Close
Can dogs swim in lakes and rivers in the Lake District?
Some dogs can safely enjoy a paddle or swim in the Lake District's lakes and rivers, but always check the conditions first. Avoid fast-flowing water, steep banks, areas with strong currents and water where blue-green algae is present for the health of your dog. Keep an eye on your dog around deep water and remember that not every stretch of lakeshore is suitable for swimming. A doggy towel is also a good idea if your pup can't resist a splash!
Close
Do dogs need to be on leads around livestock?
Yes. Dogs should be kept on a lead around livestock and under close control at all other times. The Lake District is a working landscape, so you'll often encounter sheep, cattle and other animals while out walking. Keep your dog close, follow local signage and be particularly careful during lambing season. Remember to carry a lead with you, even on walks where your dog can usually enjoy some off-lead freedom.
Close
What are the best easy dog walks in the Lake District?
For an easier dog walk in the Lake District, look for low-level routes with gentler gradients and well-maintained paths. Lakeside circuits, woodland trails and short waterfall walks are great choices for dogs who aren't used to longer or steeper hikes. Popular options from our list include Stock Ghyll Force, Grasmere Lake Circuit, Coniston Lakeshore Path and Aira Force.
Close
What should I take on a long dog walk?
For a longer Lake District walk, pack plenty of water for both you and your dog, some food or treats, poo bags and a lead. It's also worth taking a dog first-aid kit, towel, spare layers and a map or phone with the route downloaded. If you're heading onto the fells, check the weather before you leave and make sure your dog is comfortable with the distance, terrain and conditions.
